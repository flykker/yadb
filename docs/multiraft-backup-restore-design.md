# Proposed local Multi-Raft backup and restore

Design only: **none of this procedure or command surface is implemented**. Scope is the experimental three-node local OpenRaft deployment, not legacy migration or production readiness. The storage and release gates remain open (`docs/multiraft-production-todo.md`, sections 1 and 4); evidence must follow `docs/multiraft-verification.md`, “Required failure record format”.

## Proposed recovery boundary and payload

The artifact is a **whole deployment**, not a single group or node. Preserve all configured replicas for every group, including the control group, and the immutable project-to-group map. For each replica capture:

* `replica.json`, binding group, node and bootstrap voters (`openraft_runtime.rs:62–80`); the daemon's OpenRaft root/runtime marker and its configuration/manifest, without interpreting absence as a fresh root.
* `log/openraft.log`: `YORLOG04`, identity header, framed records and any version-1 checkpoint containing vote, purge boundary and retained entries (`openraft_storage.rs:22–51, 157–181`). Capture the complete active log, not just the last entry. Vote, truncations and purge/retained-suffix continuity matter to elections and replay.
* `state/state.json`: despite the name, a `YORSM001` versioned, length- and CRC-protected image containing group/node, applied log ID, stored membership, durable KV state and optional retained snapshot (`openraft_state_machine.rs:30–55, 101–117, 195–215`). Stored membership is distinct from descriptor bootstrap voters; validate both against their respective recorded values and the actual Raft history, not by assuming they are identical.
* Applied control-plane state, including project definitions and RBAC, is in the control group's KV image; preserve it and the v2 manifest's control group and project map together (`yadbd/src/main.rs:1782–1857`; `scripts/yadb-local.py:315–333`). `/v1/status` is a cached local observation, not backup evidence (`handle_status`; `docs/multiraft-idempotency-design.md`, Gate 4).

Also capture required configuration, marker, binaries and credentials, plus application data described below. The local root has `config/`, `metadata/`, `data/`, `credentials/`, `bin/`, root `state.json`, and logs (`scripts/yadb-local.py:271–274, 380–390`); root `state.json` is supervisor lifecycle state, **not** the Raft image. Preserve credential confidentiality: restrict artifact and staging permissions to owner, warn that private keys and tokens are included, and require protected storage; hashes do not encrypt or authenticate an artifact.

Each replica's log, vote, purge boundary, applied ID, retained snapshot, membership and image must form a valid recovery pair: matching identities and log IDs, no missing required replay prefix or conflicting applied boundary, and a snapshot covering any purged applied history. Across replicas, committed history and membership must agree with the quorum-confirmed cut. Do not assume equal log tails or manufacture identical replica files. **Requires change in `openraft_storage.rs` and `openraft_state_machine.rs`:** expose a read-only offline pair validator and a consensus-aware cut validator; independent existing `open`/image checks do not establish this combined property. A state-machine copy alone lacks durable vote/log and cannot establish safe recovery or membership history. The existing storage-contract and recovery gaps are explicitly open (`multiraft-production-todo.md:206–239, 438–442`).

## Proposed consistent cut and capture

**Proposed:** fence all mutation ingress on all nodes simultaneously, not one group at a time. Include control apply, SQL, S3, block, PG/iSCSI and internal/forwarded mutation paths, maintenance, membership changes and blob reclamation. Reject new mutations explicitly; drain admitted operations through their final publication or classify their outcomes unknown. Prevent a previously admitted or delayed proposal from committing after its group cut. If any ingress cannot be fenced and drained, fail the backup. A distributed admission fence with epoch/ownership, acknowledgments from every daemon and safe handling of forwarded/in-flight work is **required change in `yadbd` and the engines**; stopping only the script or issuing barriers does not provide it.

While the fence remains held, on each current group leader commit a uniquely identified Raft barrier and wait for its application and durable persistence; require current quorum and stable membership and record full log ID and membership. Then require every configured replica to apply the barrier and durably persist its image, and validate that no earlier unresolved publication can appear later. `ensure_linearizable` supplies a quorum-confirmed leader read for control today, not a write drain or global fence (`openraft_client.rs:722–769`; `multiraft-verification.md:201–217`). Sequential per-group barriers under the *common* fence represent one interval with no application mutations, not a native distributed atomic snapshot. A write acknowledged before the fence is included; an in-flight write may be included or absent if its outcome was unknown, never labeled aborted merely due to timeout. Writes rejected by the fence or acknowledged after release are outside this cut. Acceptance tests must define the acknowledgment ledger boundary accordingly.

After barriers, **proposed conservative first implementation:** cleanly shut down all Raft owners and their daemon processes while retaining the fence, await completion and exclusive locks, then capture all replica log/image pairs and data from the now-stable root. This is longer than a true online snapshot but does not pretend the current adapters can export an atomic pair. Restart the source only after an independently verified stable copy of all mutable files exists; immutable blob copies may continue after restart only if their identities are pinned against deletion for the whole copy. Otherwise keep the source stopped through the copy. Release the fence only after successful source restart and verification; on failure retain a durable fenced/incomplete state and require operator inspection, not an automatic unfence. **Requires change in `PersistentGroup`/daemon lifecycle:** coordinated barrier, all-replica apply wait, controlled shutdown and stable export; cancellation-safe startup is not established (`openraft_runtime.rs:122–159`; checklist section 3). A future proven filesystem snapshot or adapter-level atomic export can shorten downtime; it must preserve the same cut.

Live raw copying is unsafe: `persist_locked` creates `state.json.tmp`, writes bytes, `sync_data`s, renames to `state.json`, then `sync_all`s the directory (`openraft_state_machine.rs:251–300`). A copier can observe a temporary/incomplete file or a renamed image whose directory entry is not yet durable, and independently read log and state from different moments. Log appends and checkpoint replacement likewise write/sync/rename/directory-sync (`openraft_storage.rs:183–227`). Per-file atomic rename is not atomicity across files or groups. `get_current_snapshot`/`install_snapshot` and `snapshot_image_from_inner` represent Raft state, not a deployment-wide file-and-blob cut (`openraft_state_machine.rs:217–225, 581, 682`).

## Proposed blob coverage

Raft metadata is not the data itself: S3 uses upload staging and final metadata publication (`yadb-s3/src/lib.rs:192, 338, 458, 523–551`), block maps reference immutable blobs (`yadb-block/src/lib.rs:49`), and SQL prepares WAL blobs before metadata publication (`yadb-db/src/lib.rs:757–758, 863`). Blob volumes use segment files (`yadb-blob/src/volume.rs:80`). **Proposed full backup:** include all deployment-local `data/` files, including volume segments, indexes, tombstones and staging necessary for recovery, with an exhaustive inventory and hashes; validate every reference reachable from the captured metadata against the copied bytes. The fence must drain staging/publication and stop or pin GC until the copy is secure. External stores or paths must be explicitly inventoried and included via a stable, versioned copy with equivalent verification, or full backup fails; do not infer completeness from an S3 bucket listing.

**Proposed metadata-only mode:** explicitly record `coverage: metadata-only`, omitted stores and operator warning that referenced objects, SQL and block data may be unavailable. Permit inspection in an isolated offline mode only; normal serving/startup must fail closed on any missing or unverifiable referenced data, never return partial, stale or empty data as success. **Requires change in engines and daemon:** a complete reference enumerator, blob integrity validation and startup/read admission gate; existing S3, SQL and block paths do not establish this contract. The operator must supply and verify matching data through a future explicit procedure; no silent repair. S3 GC is currently disabled, not a general blob-retention guarantee (checklist current status).

## Proposed artifact and integrity

Use an exclusively created destination directory with `manifest.json`, `manifest.sha256`, `payload/`, and a final `COMPLETE` marker. Canonical UTF-8 JSON manifest version 1 records deployment ID, listener hash, original root, timestamp UTC, source node IDs and software/format versions; exact group set, cluster/group/incarnation, node, descriptor bootstrap voters, applied membership, barrier IDs, quorum results, applied/purged/last IDs and snapshot IDs; blob coverage; and every payload relative path, type, size, mode and SHA-256, including each log and image. Record a single source deployment, not just whichever leader supplied a barrier. `manifest.sha256` hashes the exact manifest bytes; `COMPLETE` binds that digest and is created only after payload, manifest and directories are synced. Exclude lock files, temp files, runtime PIDs and logs from recovery payload; preserve diagnostic logs separately as evidence. No symlinks, hard links, special files, duplicate paths, absolute paths or `..`; reject unexpected payload entries. SHA-256 detects accidental corruption but not a malicious replacement of both manifest and hash: an operator must obtain the expected digest through a trusted channel or use a separately authenticated container.

Quorum loss, timeout, failed drain, missing replica/group, unknown version, corrupt image/log, unverified blob or incomplete copy produces a retained artifact marked `INCOMPLETE` with reason and no `COMPLETE`; restore refuses it. Do not call a no-quorum capture restorable even if files look healthy. Validate exact lengths and hashes, structure, identity, membership, all barriers and log/state consistency before publication; reject truncated payload, unknown formats, extra/missing groups, mismatched identity/listener hash or missing credentials. `deployment_ports` currently allows a legacy warning when both identity files are missing (`scripts/yadb-local.py:50–75`): **requires change** for backup/restore and restored startup to refuse that fallback.

## Proposed restore into a new root

1. Require an explicit trusted expected deployment ID and manifest digest; verify the entire artifact and `COMPLETE` before writing the target. Require the original deployment and all replicas to be stopped and fenced, all associated listeners free, and no other copy of this identity able to run. Same identity, node IDs, voters, listener addresses/hash and credentials are required: this is disaster recovery, not cloning. Local port checks alone cannot prove absence of another host or a paused process; require operator fencing and exclusive supervisor and replica ownership locks. Fail if this cannot be established. Never restore over a live or existing nonempty root; reserve a previously nonexistent target with exclusive `mkdir`, reject symlinks and hold a restore lock.
2. Write a durable `RESTORE_INCOMPLETE` marker first, then copy payload using create-exclusive files, restrictive modes, length/hash verification, file sync and bottom-up directory sync. A crash or failure leaves that target and marker untouched for inspection; a repeat refuses it, never cleans or resumes automatically. The artifact is read-only. Validate identity and pair invariants offline without invoking an opener that mutates files: `DurableOpenRaftLog::open` removes a temporary file (`openraft_storage.rs:489–496`).
3. Rewrite only root-dependent absolute paths in group configs and node manifests to the new root; preserve addresses, deployment and group identities, map, voter lists and credentials. Set all replicas `mode: open`, `initialize: false` and root lifecycle state to `stopped`, never `prepared`. Recompute listener hash and compare to the original, and validate marker, descriptor, complete replica/group set, binary compatibility and TLS credentials. The script currently writes absolute paths and switches modes on restart (`scripts/yadb-local.py:321–331, 394–413`). **Requires change:** a narrowly validated path rewrite and a startup guard that refuses an incomplete restore before any create/open or listener bind.
4. Validate all referenced blob data for a full restore; metadata-only remains isolated and non-serving. Sync rewritten files and directories, atomically replace the incomplete marker with a durable restore-complete record bound to manifest digest, then sync the root and parent. Only then may an operator explicitly start in open-only mode and verify leaders, membership, cut IDs and application data. On any error preserve files and evidence; no bootstrap, reinitialization, fallback to legacy, auto-repair or overwrite. Existing `PersistentGroup::open` fails missing/invalid files, whereas `create` reserves fresh directories (`openraft_runtime.rs:82–135`); the new guard must prevent choosing create for a partial restore.

## Proposed operator surface and evidence

Prefer `scripts/yadb-local.py backup <root> <dest> [--metadata-only]` and `restore <backup> <newroot> --expected-deployment-id ID --manifest-sha256 HASH`: the script owns this local root, process supervision and listener/identity preflight, while `yadbctl` is a client and cannot safely own local files or processes. The script must call new authenticated daemon/storage primitives, not implement Raft consistency by copying live paths. Artifact and restore target are never overwritten. Proposed exit codes: 0 verified complete, 2 usage/precondition/identity, 3 quorum/fence/incomplete, 4 integrity/format, 5 I/O or unexpected failure; print artifact path and digest or retained incomplete path and reason, never secrets.

Write a non-secret evidence record adjacent to each artifact/target: command and source revision/diff, host/filesystem, timestamps, topology/identities/voters, fence and drain acknowledgments, per-group leader/quorum/barrier/applied/purge positions, inclusion boundary, blob coverage, hashes, failures and restore/start observations. For failures also record exact injected boundary, command IDs, acknowledged versus unknown outcomes, recovered state, availability and paths to retained raw artifacts (`multiraft-verification.md:519–526`). Protect any evidence that could reveal sensitive paths or topology. **Requires change:** no such command, fence, validator or record writer exists today.

## Limits and rejected alternatives

No point-in-time recovery between completed cuts: no archived incremental log/consistent blob history exists; PITR is future work. Cross-cluster migration and identity/address changes are outside this same-identity protocol and require a separate fencing/import design, not a flag. No guarantee exists for a cut when any group lacks quorum; refusal is inherent to this safety contract. External side effects are not rolled back or captured, an inherent boundary without separately coordinated systems. Loss of a source quorum or corrupted source files may make backup unavailable; successful hashes are not proof of consensus correctness or power-loss safety.

Rejected: `cp -a` of live data races storage transitions and misses a cross-group cut; only `state.json` omits log/vote/identity and blobs; overwrite of an existing root risks mixing histories or split brain; a Raft snapshot alone omits replica log/vote, deployment configuration and external blobs; an S3 bucket copy alone omits control, SQL/block and Raft state and does not establish a matching cut.

## Proposed gate acceptance criteria

Use disposable three-process local clusters and an external acknowledgment ledger. Start concurrent writes before backup, track fence admission and successful responses, restore the full artifact into a new root after fencing the source, elect leaders in every group and verify every write acknowledged before the fence plus each admitted successful write through the cut, with exact atomic results and referenced bytes. Check post-release source writes are not promised; classify unknown outcomes rather than asserting abort. Exercise forwarded/delayed writes and control/data dependencies while the fence is established. Verify log/state pair checks and all nine replica identities, voters, membership and barriers.

Independently assert: mismatched expected identity/listener is refused; truncated or missing payload and altered manifest are refused; a nonempty target is untouched; a metadata-only artifact with a dangling blob cannot serve it or report successful partial data; a group lacking quorum yields an explicitly incomplete artifact that restore refuses. Inject interruption at marker, copy, sync, rewrite and completion boundaries; partial roots remain untouched on retry and cannot start or bootstrap. Test stale processes, held locks, path traversal, duplicate entries and unavailable blobs. Preserve the required failure records and raw artifacts; distinguish validation from successful service availability.

Passing establishes only the tested local process/filesystem scenarios and defined cut, not power loss, hardware/filesystem durability, arbitrary failures, multi-host fencing, cross-cluster migration, PITR or external effects (`multiraft-verification.md:509–517`). The adapted storage suite and existing process tests do not close this gate.
